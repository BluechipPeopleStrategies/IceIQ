The success and growth of hockey at the grassroots level in Wisconsin has the fingerprints of Don Bradford all over it. A player himself who fell in love with the game on the frozen ponds of the Badger State. Bradford has devoted the last five decades to the game that's given him so much. Working with a passion, dedication, and humility to making hockey in Wisconsin at all levels what it is today. Bradford's lifelong passion for hockey began as a young player before transitioning to coach, hockey parent, and volunteer as the years passed. Raised in Eagle River, Wisconsin, Bradford played his youth and high school hockey in one of the oldest programs in the state, winning the unofficial Wisconsin State High School Tournament in 1964 before continuing his journey at the University of Wisconsin-Superior. After two years of military service, Bradford began his coaching career at Wausau West High School in 1975, where he also taught business and accounting. It was during his three seasons behind the bench for Wausau that he recognized the need to strengthen grassroots programming in Wisconsin, ultimately shifting his focus towards youth development and expanding access to the sport across the region. In the decades that followed, Bradford became one of the foundational leaders of youth hockey in the area, making it his mission to provide programming, development opportunities, and equipment to local associations. Through leadership roles with Marathon County Youth Hockey and Wisconsin Amateur Hockey Association, Bradford's work shaped the sport. He partnered with local municipalities to build additional rinks, many of which he helped construct with his own two hands, and teamed up with philanthropic partners to secure almost half a million dollars of equipment, spearheading efforts to donate thousands of pieces of hockey equipment to local associations. Bradford's commitment to accessibility opened the door for children across Wisconsin to experience hockey for the first time. Inducted into the Wisconsin Hockey Hall of Fame in 2009, Bradford's legacy is cemented in the players he's welcomed into the hockey family, coaches he's mentored, and relationships he's built on and off the ice. To this day, Bradford can still be found welcoming children into the rink, working with partners to distribute equipment across the state, or taking the ice himself to help instill the love of the game in the next generation. The foundation of his commitment to growing the sport. Please join us in congratulating the 2026 William Thayer Tutt Award winner, Don Bradford.
