Mark Carlson's success isn't defined solely by the wins on the ice. While the victories have been rewarding, it's the personal growth and development of the thousands of athletes he's coached and mentored through his career that truly motivates the legendary coach each and every day. A native of Lawrenceville, New Jersey, Carlson's journey in hockey began as a player, carrying his excitement for the game to UMass Lowell before transitioning into the coaching ranks, embarking on what has been a celebrated career behind the bench. After assistant roles at Lowell and Northeastern, he found a new home with the Cedar Rapids RoughRiders of the United States Hockey League in 1999. A place where he's built one of the most remarkable coaching resumes in junior hockey history. Over his 26-year tenure as head coach and general manager, he's coached in a USHL record 1,532 games. And on October 3rd, 2025, recorded his 779th win, the most regular season victories in league With Carlson at the helm, Cedar Rapids has taken home the Anderson Cup as the USHL's regular season champions in 2005, 2011, and 2016, reached the Clark Cup playoffs 20 times, and posted a winning record in all but six of his campaigns. One of the most storied seasons for Carlson and the RoughRiders came in 2004-2005, where he guided the team to a program record 42 wins before leading the team to its first-ever Clark Cup championship. His leadership has been recognized across the league, having been named Coach of the Year four times, including this season. On the international stage, he steered Team USA to back-to-back World Junior A Challenge titles in 2008 and 2009, jump-starting a run for the red, white, and blue that included six tournament titles in seven years. The first and only coach in RoughRiders history, Carlson's legacy extends far beyond the scoreboard.
