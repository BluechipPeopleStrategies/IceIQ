# Visible-Cue Prediction Design

Predict-next questions must not ask learners to infer an opponent's hidden
decision or intelligence. The visible frame must establish a committed motion,
then the learner predicts its geometric consequence.

For the 2-on-1 defender-step proof play, show that D1 has stopped backing in and
has begun stepping toward F1 before the freeze. Ask what happens to the passing
lane next. The lane behind D1 opens because D1 cannot step to F1 and cover F2
simultaneously.

Reusable rule:

- Hidden decision: ask what the player **should** do from that player's POV.
- Visible commitment: ask what space, lane, or option changes next.
- Never ask what an opponent "does next" when multiple choices depend on
  unknown intent rather than visible evidence.

Future variants may show a visible sag into the pass lane or continued retreat,
then ask the same consequence-based question.

Tests reject hidden-intent wording in predict-next prompts and require visible
commitment language in this proof play.
