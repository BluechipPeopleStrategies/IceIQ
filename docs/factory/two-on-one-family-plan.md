# 2-on-1 Scenario Family Plan

The 2-on-1 family should teach players to read the defender, goalie, support teammate, and recovery pressure.

## Family Goal

Players learn that a 2-on-1 is not automatically a pass.

The correct play depends on:

- defender commitment
- pass lane
- shot lane
- goalie position
- support angle
- recovery pressure

## Current / Target Variants

### 1. Defender steps up

Cue:

- defender commits to puck carrier

Read:

- pass to support teammate

Reveal:

- support teammate is open for a quick shot

### 2. Defender holds middle

Cue:

- defender protects pass lane

Read:

- attack shot lane

Reveal:

- forcing a pass through coverage is low value

### 3. Backchecker closing

Cue:

- recovery pressure is arriving

Read:

- move puck before the lane closes

Reveal:

- waiting allows the backchecker to kill the play

### 4. Support option too flat

Cue:

- support teammate is not in a dangerous passing lane

Read:

- do not force the pass

Reveal:

- puck carrier may need to shoot, delay with purpose, or attack space

### 5. Goalie late to slide

Cue:

- goalie is not square after lateral puck movement

Read:

- quick shot

Reveal:

- timing matters after the pass

### 6. Defender takes pass away

Cue:

- defender fully removes pass lane

Read:

- puck carrier keeps attack or shoots

Reveal:

- the threat of the pass creates space for the puck carrier

## Factory Notes

Each variant should ask one main question, then reveal the consequence.

Do not add a second question unless a new visible cue changes the decision.

Telemetry must capture the player-facing question, answer options, reveal text, and cue text for each age band.


### 6. Pass lane removed

Cue:

- defender fully removes the pass lane

Read:

- puck carrier attacks the open shot lane instead of forcing the pass

Reveal:

- a 2-on-1 does not always mean pass; the defender's choice creates the next best option
