# RinkReads: Viability + MVP Recommendations

Beyond the engine and the gauntlet, here is what makes the product viable, and the popular-game mechanics worth borrowing for the MVP.

## The moat (lead with this)
1. **Correctness by construction.** The read-solver computes every answer from geometry, so a wrong answer key is structurally impossible. No competitor hand-authoring or LLM-guessing can promise that. Market it plainly: "every read is verified, not guessed." For a kids' app, trust is the product.
2. **A content library that compounds.** Authored plays + the solver generate thousands of verified, age-laddered items. That library is a defensible data asset that grows cheaper per item over time.

## Distribution (the biggest growth lever)
3. **B2B2C through teams and associations.** Sell the Team tier to one coach and you onboard a 15-20 kid roster at once. Coaches assign lessons and get a report. This is the cheapest acquisition channel by far. Seed it: give a few teams a free season for testimonials.
4. **Daily Read (the Wordle model).** One free, shareable scenario per day with a streak and a share card ("read it in 4s"). Zero friction, viral, builds a daily habit. This is the single highest-ROI MVP feature: cheap to build (one solver-keyed item/day), top-of-funnel growth, and it pulls lapsed users back.

## Retention (mechanics to borrow from popular games)
5. **Puzzle Rush (Chess.com).** Solve as many reads as you can before a few misses or a timer. Highly replayable, leaderboard-friendly, ideal for U13+.
6. **Streaks + leagues (Duolingo).** The progression spine, age-gated per the Duolingo design (puck-fill and characters for U7, leagues for U15+).
7. **Daily brain-training drills + a performance graph (Elevate / Peak / Lumosity).** Frame the scan-and-count and reaction-time drills as a daily "hockey-brain workout" for older players, with a simple mastery heatmap.
8. **Head-to-head / pass-and-play.** Challenge a teammate to the same read set. Social pressure drives daily use.

## Real-world fit
9. **Offline / PWA.** Rinks have terrible wifi. An installable, offline-capable app so kids can train rink-side is a real unlock that most edtech ignores.
10. **Parent and coach reports.** The report card is what parents pay for and what coaches assign. It is also the re-engagement loop (a daily parent summary for young kids).
11. **Real-game transfer hook.** "I saw that read in my game this week." Let a player flag a real-game read, tie the training to actual games. This is the emotional payoff that makes it stick.

## Credibility
12. **ADM alignment + a named coach endorsement.** Map the curriculum to USA Hockey / Hockey Canada ADM and get one known coach or association to validate it. Combined with "computed by geometry, reviewed by coaches," that is a strong trust claim.

## Recommended MVP cut (do not boil the ocean)
- One age band fully (suggest U11), 2-3 units of the skill tree.
- The play engine + solver + the gauntlet + 3-4 question types (MC, pick-the-space, what-happens-next, binary speed read).
- The Duolingo-lite loop: streak, XP, daily goal.
- The Daily Read (free, shareable) as the growth hook.
- One coach/team as a design partner.

Ship that, get a real roster using it daily, then widen the age bands and add Puzzle Rush + leagues.
