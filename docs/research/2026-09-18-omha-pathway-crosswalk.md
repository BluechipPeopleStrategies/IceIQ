## Existing modules: keep scope, clarify meaning

- **Rink spots:** teach names and recognition first. A full-rink learning diagram does not assert that every age plays full-ice games. The OMHA historical implementation notice differentiates U7 cross-ice from U9 half-ice and describes a later U9 transition. It is dated December 23, 2019; do not present its dates or dimensions as current universal policy. [Historical OMHA notice](https://www.omha.net/news_article/show/1073503-u7-ip-u9-novice-and-u11-atom-player-pathway-implementation)
- **Six role labels and heatmaps:** preserve the requested labels. Label shaded areas as examples of where a role may be seen; actual positioning changes with the play, rink format and coach's approach. Hockey Canada's U9 guidance recommends goalie rotation as well. [U9 guide](https://hockeycanada.ca/en-ca/hockey-programs/coaching/under-9)
- **Gear drag/drop:** retain as equipment vocabulary and recognition. These pathway sources do not validate a dressing sequence, protective fit or safety certification. Any such added claims need their own appropriate source and human review.
- **Referee study/matching:** retain as signal recognition. Do not imply that identifying a gesture proves understanding of when a penalty applies. Signal artwork, wording and rule examples require a separate current rulebook check; this crosswalk has not approved them.

## Limits for implementation

Hockey Canada's published practice-time allocations concern **on-ice practice**, not screen activities; do not convert them into app percentages or time requirements. The transferable structures above are original product proposals, not official digital curriculum, copied drills or evidence of improved on-ice performance.

## Short learner guidance (original draft)

**U7:** Explore one thing at a time. Find a rink spot, name a piece of gear, or look at a hockey role with a grown-up. Try again whenever you like. Learning the words is one small part of learning to play.

**U9:** Revisit familiar rink spots, try the gear match, and explore every role. The role pictures are examples, and players move as the game changes. Ask your coach how the words and pictures connect to the hockey you play.

**U11:** Compare the roles, explain which clue helped you find a rink spot, and practise recognizing referee signals. A diagram or signal match is a starting point. Talk with your coach about how it applies to a real play.
