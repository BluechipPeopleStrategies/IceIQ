About these illustrations & teaching sources

This is a full-ice, five-skater-plus-goalie orientation example. It is not a required U7/U9 game format. Younger players should explore landmarks and try different roles; positions are not permanent assignments. Left and right mean the player’s left and right while facing the opponent’s net. On this board, left wing is at the top.

Heat maps are hand-authored illustrations of possible responsibility emphasis, not measured skating paths, probabilities, percentages or universal tactical assignments. Uncoloured ice is still available. Actual locations depend on puck location, teammates, opponents, the team system and the stage of play. Transition has separate examples for winning and losing possession. No map awards mastery or grades a player.

Painted markings and coaching terms are separated. Tactical areas have approximate boundaries. Bench and official-area locations are schematic. This prototype uses the project’s existing NHL-size metric rink; rink dimensions and markings vary across facilities and rule sets.

Sources inform general orientation and role principles; they do not validate these exact shaded regions. Content and maps remain drafts for a named human coach before curriculum admission.

Learn the signal, not a penalty length. How a call is handled depends on the age group, game format and local rules. U7/U9 small-area games are different from full-ice hockey. Ask your coach or official about your program.

Signal source & review boundary

Original simplified illustrations based on Hockey Canada Playing Rules 2026–2028, printed pages 2–3. Captions explain motion that a still picture cannot show. This starter set avoids signals that need more game context, such as a raised arm for a delayed call. A named qualified official must review the drawings and explanations before pilot use. Review notes & source fidelity

These are four layout storyboards, using the complete six-question U7 and U11 companion lessons, version 2. Wording, options, answer keys, explanations, initial actor positions and objectives come directly from the source file. The rink is a schematic using the canonical metric profile, not a new 3D asset.

The U11 branch changes the feedback route only. Hypothetical changes remain verbal; no outcome is simulated. Placement and reflection receive authored coaching feedback, never an invented grade. Sequence answers remain a suggested routine. Experimental lesson availability is not human coaching approval or mastery eligibility.

Each layout begins with fresh responses. Switching layouts resets that layout's local attempt. No response leaves this page and nothing is stored after reload. For a fair learner trial, alternate which layout is seen first and use separate learners or equivalent lesson pairs to reduce recall effects. Compare help needed, missed cues, recovery and later transfer; completion time alone is insufficient.
