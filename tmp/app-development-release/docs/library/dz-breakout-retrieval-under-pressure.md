# D-zone breakout: retrieval under forecheck pressure

**Family:** `dz_breakout` · **Concept:** `dz-breakout` · **Bands:** U11, U13, U15, U18

## The read

A defender retrieves the puck behind their own net as the first forechecker closes.
The forechecker has to commit to one side of the net to arrive with speed. The moment
they commit, they can no longer defend the other side — the net itself is in their way,
and their hips are turned.

**The read: take the puck away from the side the forechecker committed to.**

This is the same underlying principle as the take-away-the-reverse forecheck play
(`play_forecheck_pressure_take_away_reverse_u13_v1`), seen from the other side of the
puck. There, the forechecker learns to angle so the escape is closed. Here, the
retrieving defender learns to leave before it is.

## Why the wrong answers are wrong

| Option | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Continue toward the pressure | You are skating into a player arriving with more speed than you have. Behind your own net is the worst place on the ice to lose a puck battle. |
| Straight out in front of your own net | The slot is the most dangerous ice on the sheet. A retrieval that goes through your own slot turns a routine breakout into a scoring chance against. This is the cardinal error and worth naming plainly. |
| Hold it behind the net and wait | The second forechecker is already arriving. Waiting turns one problem into two, and the outlet that was open when you picked the puck up will not still be there. |

None of these is defensible given what the player can see. That is deliberate: a
distractor that is arguably correct teaches a child that their good read was wrong.

## Age framing

- **U11 / U13:** "Go the way they can't turn." Concrete and spatial.
- **U15 / U18:** The forechecker's commitment is a *cue*, and the escape is *pre-scanned* —
  the defender should already know which way is open before the puck is on their stick.
  Connects to `scanning.md`.

## Coverage note

This is the first play in the `dz_breakout` family. Planned siblings:

1. **Winger on the pressure side.** Same picture, but the outlet support sits on the side
   the forechecker committed to. The correct answer stays "escape away from pressure" and
   the available teammate becomes a red herring — this isolates the pressure cue from the
   support cue, which the first play deliberately keeps aligned.
2. **Forechecker takes away the escape.** The forechecker angles across the top of the
   net instead of down one side; now the reverse or the rim is the read.
3. **Second forechecker cheats the wall.** The outlet is covered; the read becomes the
   middle-lane support or the glass-and-out.
4. **Verdict kind.** Watch a retrieval and judge whether the defender picked the right
   escape, then justify from a visible cue.
